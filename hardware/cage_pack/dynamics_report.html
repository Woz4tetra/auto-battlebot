Model and its limits

  • Sheets: 1/4" polycarbonate, 2.44 x 1.22 m (NHRL wiki, 3 lb cage), E 2.38 GPa, held in channels on all four sides with $engage mm of engagement (assumed). The edges slide in plane, so both sheets are linear Kirchhoff plates, simply supported, 200 modes to ~600 Hz, 3 % damping. hardware/cage_pack/dynamics.py and two_sheet.py.
  • Pack: rigid, three DOF (bounce, rock about X and Y) on two cups, each an axial spring and a disk's rocking spring (k R2/4) against the sheet's slope, with 10 % damping; shear treated as rigid. The printed PETG panel is about 5 times less stiff in bending than the 1.6 mm aluminium one (E 2.1 vs 69 GPa, 3 vs 1.6 mm thick), before its ribs; it and the printed cup arms flex between the cups and act as a softer cup spring, which moves the result toward the soft end of the slider.
  • Robot hits: two_sheet.py models the robot as a 1.36 kg point mass on the inner sheet and the sheet-to-sheet contact on a 61 x 31 grid, and its transferred momentum drives the outer-sheet pulse here. It runs without the pack. The spinner bite keeps a 50 % guess; the last row puts all the momentum through.
  • Rubber friction in the channels holds the edges a little, so real bending sits slightly under these numbers.
  • SmallRig's ratings are static. Vacuum cups hold short pulls better than long ones, but they fail by edge peel, which this model does not resolve.
